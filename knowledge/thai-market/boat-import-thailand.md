## Identity

Thailand offers one of the most favourable regulatory environments in Asia for yacht ownership. Since a landmark 2004 reform, recreational vessel imports carry **0% import duty**, making Thailand significantly more attractive than neighbouring countries. Understanding the duty/VAT/registration framework is essential for advising international buyers.

## Import Duty: 0%

Since 18 February 2004, the Thai Customs Department has exempted recreational vessels from import duty and excise tax. Categories covered include inflatables, sailboats, and motor yachts used for recreation. This applies to vessels imported for private use or commercial charter operation. Per Thai Customs Department (sail-world.com report, Feb 2004).

**Note:** This 0% duty applies to recreational vessels specifically. Commercial vessels, fishing boats, and passenger ships may be subject to different rates. A customs broker should confirm classification before import.

## VAT: 7%

Thailand's standard VAT rate of **7%** applies to the CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) value of imported yachts. This is not waived and must be paid at the point of import. The 7% VAT rate has been in effect since 2023 (previously there were discussions about raising it to 10%). Per Thai Revenue Department, vatupdate.com.

**Effective cost:** For a USD 1M yacht, the total import tax is approximately USD 70K (VAT only) — compared to 20-30% combined duty+VAT in many other Asian countries.

## Registration Options

**Thai Flag:**
- Requires 70% Thai ownership via a Thai company structure
- Common approach: Thai company with Thai shareholders holding majority, foreign investor holding minority
- Thai-flagged vessels benefit from simplified domestic navigation and port clearance
- Professional legal advice is essential for company structuring

**Offshore Flags (common for Asian owners):**
- **Marshall Islands** — popular for superyachts globally; straightforward registration, favourable tax treatment
- **Cayman Islands** — well-established for Asian-owned yachts; strong legal framework
- **British Virgin Islands (BVI)** — common for Asian family office structures; often already used for holding companies
- Offshore-flagged yachts can operate in Thai waters under temporary import permits

Per Yacht Style, Thai maritime law firms, Asia Pacific Boating.

## Foreign Charter License

Thailand allows foreign-flagged yachts to operate commercially under a charter license:
- Minimum vessel size: **24m LOA** (lowered from 30m in a recent reform to encourage more charter activity)
- License duration: 1 year, renewable
- Thai crew is not mandatory but recommended
- Foreign charter yachts may stay in Thai waters for up to 2.5 years (with extensions possible for 30m+ vessels)

Per Thai Maritime Department, Yacht Style, Asia Pacific Boating.

## Key Considerations

- **No annual vessel tax** for Thai-flagged recreational boats (unlike many European countries)
- **Crew/work permits**: Foreign crew working on Thai-based yachts need appropriate work permits; the yacht's flag state and the crew's nationality determine the process
- **Marina fees**: Not regulated — vary significantly by marina, season, and vessel size
- **Insurance**: Thai-based insurers exist but many owners use international marine insurers (Lloyd's, Pantaenius)
