## Summary (one paragraph)

The European yacht show circuit is the global centre of gravity for the yacht industry, where most new production models launch and superyacht deals are brokered. Per YachtBuyer, BOAT International, and Y.CO (2026), the circuit peaks in September with Cannes and Monaco, supplemented by boot Dusseldorf in January (world's largest indoor boat show), Palma in spring, and Genoa in autumn. For Asian buyers and dealers, Europe is where they source the brands later exhibited at TBF — making the European calendar directly relevant to TBF's exhibitor pipeline. Many Asian HNWIs also attend Cannes and Monaco personally.

## Relevance to TBF / Sand

**How European shows connect to TBF:**

1. **Exhibitor pipeline:** TBF brands (Sunseeker, Azimut, Princess, Sanlorenzo, Wally) premiere models at Cannes, Monaco, or Genoa. By TBF in January, these are available for Asian debut.
2. **Asian buyer travel:** Thai, HK, and Singaporean buyers attend Cannes and Monaco personally, placing orders in September for TBF display the following January.
3. **Dealer attendance:** TBF's key dealers (DCH Marine, BLY, Simpson Marine, Derani) attend European shows to meet manufacturers and scout brands.
4. **Monaco's Asian push:** Asia-Pacific destinations promoted at Monaco 2025 (per Nautispots), signalling European interest in Asia. TBF benefits.
5. **boot Dusseldorf** is less relevant for TBF's luxury positioning but important for smaller categories (Axopar, Saxdor) and marine tech.

**Key dynamics:**
- Cannes and Monaco are back-to-back in September — a two-week Riviera "yacht season"
- Monaco: strictly superyacht (30m+); 116 yachts in 2025 (per YachtBuyer)
- Cannes: broader (10m–40m+), boats under 10m on land, larger in-water
- Genoa (October): best for Italian yards (Sanlorenzo, Azimut-Benetti, Ferretti)
- Palma (spring): growing rapidly; Superyacht Village introduced 2025
