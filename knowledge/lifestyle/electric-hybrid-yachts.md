## Summary

The electric and hybrid yacht sector is undergoing rapid transformation, headlined by the April 2026 launch of E-Lektra Marine, a joint venture between Groupe Beneteau and Fountaine Pajot Group electrifying seven sailing brands. From solar catamarans to electric hydrofoil tenders, the technology is maturing quickly, though marina charging infrastructure in Asia lags behind Europe. For TBF yacht owners, electrification is becoming a genuine purchasing consideration.

## Key Information

**Major Developments (2024-2026)**

In April 2026, Groupe Beneteau and Fountaine Pajot Group announced E-Lektra Marine, a joint venture covering seven brands: Beneteau, Jeanneau, Lagoon, Excess, Dufour, Fountaine Pajot Sailing Catamarans, and Fountaine Pajot Yachts. Per Plugboats, April 2026, the JV aims to bring electric propulsion to 10-15% of the global sailing market by 2030, with solutions for boats from 9 to 24 metres plus retrofit options.

Axopar launched its AX/E Electric line with Evoy motors: the AX/E 25 (125 kWh, 305 hp, approximately 27 nm range) and AX/E 22 (63 kWh, 122 hp). Per Plugboats, 2025, prices start around USD 140,000.

**Solar and Hybrid Catamarans**

Sunreef Yachts' Power Eco range integrates solar panels into the hull and superstructure for silent, emission-free propulsion. Silent Yachts (Austria) builds solar catamarans capable of transoceanic crossings with zero fuel under optimal conditions. Per Sunreef Yachts, 2025.

Greenline Yachts (Slovenia), a hybrid pioneer, won the 2025 Readers' Choice "Favorite Green Boat" award. Greenline reported three recent Southeast Asian sales, with dealer Hakan Lange noting "green energy is very much a hot topic." Per Yacht Style, 2025.

**Electric Tenders and Hydrofoils**

Candela's electric hydrofoil P-12 ferry completed a 160 nm Mediterranean crossing in April 2026, the first all-electric boat to do so. Per Plugboats, April 2026. For yacht tenders, Torqeedo and ePropulsion dominate, with models from small dinghy motors to 80 hp equivalents.

**Regulatory Drivers**

At MEPC 83 in April 2025, the IMO approved a Net-Zero Framework for global shipping, set for formal adoption in October 2025. Per IMO, 2025, the EU has separately incorporated maritime transport into its Emissions Trading System.

## Asian / Thai Market Context

Marina charging infrastructure in Thailand remains limited. Most Phuket marinas offer standard shore power (16A-63A), but dedicated DC fast-charging for electric vessels is not yet available. Per Helios Marine, 2025, Asian marinas are years behind European counterparts like Amsterdam, which is electrifying its entire canal fleet.

However, Thailand's tropical climate is an advantage: abundant sunlight makes solar vessels like Sunreef Eco and Silent Yachts compelling. A solar catamaran in Phuket can generate meaningful energy year-round, partially offsetting the charging gap.

Greenline's Southeast Asian sales suggest growing regional interest. The global electric boat market is projected to reach approximately USD 25 billion by 2034. Per Fortune Business Insights, 2025.
