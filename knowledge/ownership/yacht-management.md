## Summary

Yacht management companies provide the operational backbone for yacht ownership, handling everything from crew hiring and payroll to regulatory compliance, maintenance scheduling, and charter revenue management. In Asia, a mix of global firms with regional offices and local specialists serve the market, with Phuket emerging as a key hub for management services in Southeast Asia.

## Key Information

**What a Yacht Management Company Does**

A full-service yacht management company handles: crew recruitment, training, payroll, and rotation; maintenance and repair coordination; regulatory compliance including flag state surveys, class renewals, and MCA coding; financial management including budgeting and owner reporting; charter management including marketing, booking, and revenue optimization; and insurance coordination. Per Foreland Marine (2025), the industry "10% rule" suggests annual running costs approximate 10% of the yacht's value, though realistic ranges are 7-15% depending on vessel age, usage, and crew size. Crew costs represent 35-45% of annual expenditure, followed by berthing, insurance, fuel, and a refit reserve of 2-4%.

**Key Players in Asia**

Global firms with strong Asian presence include: Burgess (Hong Kong and Singapore offices, 45+ years experience, per Yacht Style, 2025); IYC (International Yacht Collection) with Asia offices offering sales, charter, and management; Northrop & Johnson with a Phuket office (per Yacht Style, 2025); Camper & Nicholsons with Phuket presence and Asian coverage; and Asia Pacific Superyachts (APSA), Singapore-based, offering agency, berthing, customs, and logistics support across the region.

**Phuket-Based Management**

Phuket hosts a cluster of management operators: PMYA Asia provides charter management, owner and crew services, and relocation. Faraway Yachting offers transparent management with owner reporting. Phuket Yacht Management and Repair (PYMaR) handles technical management and repair coordination. Ao Po Grand Marina and Royal Phuket Marina serve as home bases for many managed vessels. Camper & Nicholsons and Northrop & Johnson both maintain Phuket offices.

**Typical Management Fees**

Per industry sources (2024-2025), full management typically costs 5-10% of yacht value annually, or a flat monthly fee of roughly USD 2,000-8,000 for vessels in the 20-40m range. Charter management (marketing and booking) is usually 15-20% of charter revenue. Crew management alone may be USD 500-2,000 monthly. Compliance and certification costs are typically billed separately, per Southern Right Yachting (2025).

**Remote Monitoring Systems**

Modern yacht management increasingly relies on remote monitoring. Yacht Sentinel offers sensor-based monitoring of bilge levels, battery voltage, shore power, temperature, and unauthorized access via mobile app. Per Yachting Monthly (2024), the YS6L system provides comprehensive remote oversight. Similar platforms include Siren Marine and Sentinel Marine, which integrate with digital switching systems on newer yachts.

## Asian / Thai Market Context

Many Phuket-based owners use a hybrid model: a local captain handles day-to-day operations while a management company provides compliance, charter marketing, and refit project management. Thailand's growing reputation as a winter cruising destination has increased demand for management services, particularly for owners who leave yachts in Phuket during the off-season (May-October).

## Talking Points for Sand

- For owners new to the region, recommend a full-service management company to navigate Thai regulations and logistics.
- Management fees are an investment -- poor management leads to accelerated depreciation and costly emergency repairs.
- Remote monitoring systems are a low-cost complement worth recommending to any owner leaving their yacht unattended.
- Be transparent that charter revenue rarely covers all costs but can significantly offset them.
- Suggest owners interview at least two management companies and request references from clients with similar-sized vessels.

## DO NOT

- Recommend a specific company as "the best" -- suitability depends on vessel type, budget, and preferences.
- Promise that charter income will cover management fees entirely.
- Share details about specific yacht owners' arrangements or fleet rosters.
- Underestimate Thai maritime regulations' complexity for foreign-owned vessels.
