## Summary

Phuket is Southeast Asia's leading yacht refit destination, offering marinas, hardstand facilities, and specialist workshops at costs significantly below Singapore and Australia. Ongoing investment in facilities such as Yacht Solutions' superyacht-capable yard is expanding Phuket's ability to handle vessels up to 95 meters.

## Key Information

**Marina Facilities**

Phuket Boat Lagoon (opened 1994) offers approximately 180 wet berths with travel lift capacity for vessels up to around 35 meters. Per Phuket Marine Guide (2023), the marina hosts independent workshops, marine electricians, riggers, and fibreglass specialists. Yacht Haven Marina (northeast coast) provides haul-out via ramp for vessels up to approximately 30m, with on-site contractors for mechanical, electrical, and cosmetic work. Royal Phuket Marina offers a 50-ton travel lift and hardstand for yachts up to approximately 25 meters, with convenient access to Phuket Town suppliers.

**Key Refit Yards and Specialists**

Ratanachai Shipyard is one of Phuket's most established refit facilities with a 52-meter, 210-ton slipway. Per Superyacht Times (2025), Ratanachai partners with major Phuket marinas and management companies. Yacht Solutions (YSITM), a joint venture with Italthai Marine, operates with lifting capacity for vessels up to 95 meters -- a major upgrade in Phuket's superyacht capability. South Sea Marine offers a travel lift up to 80 tonnes. Specialist firms including Precision Shipwright Services and ClearVac Engineering Asia handle everything from teak deck replacement to engine overhauls.

**Common Refit Work**

Frequently requested services include: antifouling and below-waterline maintenance (typically annual); engine and generator servicing; teak deck sanding, caulking, and replacement; hull painting and fibreglass repair; electronics upgrades; rigging inspection (sailing yachts); interior refurbishment; and HVAC maintenance.

**Cost Comparison**

Per Asia Global Yachting (2025), labor and materials in Asia are typically 40-60% more affordable than Europe or Australia. Per Simpson Marine (2025), Singapore is the region's most expensive refit destination. Johor and Batam offer lower costs with variable quality oversight. Phuket sits in a "balanced pricing" position -- more affordable than Singapore, with better project management than Batam. Imported parts remain a cost factor across Asian yards.

**Timing**

The optimal haul-out window is the wet season (May-October), when yachts are less likely in active use. Off-season scheduling improves yard availability and pricing. Antifouling should be refreshed annually, often aligned with pre-dry-season preparation (September-October) for the November-April cruising season.

## Asian / Thai Market Context

Phuket's refit industry has matured significantly, driven by the growing regional yacht fleet. The island's skilled Thai craftsmen, international project managers, and competitive pricing make it the default choice for Andaman Sea owners. However, per Noonsite (2024), Phuket is "no longer cheap for refitting" compared to a decade ago, and imported components carry significant markups. For the largest superyachts (60m+), some owners still prefer Singapore or European yards for major projects requiring covered facilities and class oversight.

## Talking Points for Sand

- Recommend visiting shortlisted yards in person before committing to a refit project.
- Emphasize detailed refit specifications and project management -- scope creep causes budget overruns.
- Suggest aligning haul-out with the wet season for best availability and value.
- For yachts 50m+, flag that Phuket's covered facilities are still developing.
- Recommend obtaining at least three quotes and checking references.

## DO NOT

- Guarantee refit timelines -- weather, parts availability, and scope changes cause delays.
- Present Phuket as uniformly cheaper -- imported parts and specialized labor narrow the gap.
- Recommend contractors without noting quality varies and due diligence is essential.
- Suggest dry-season haul-out is impossible -- it is feasible but less optimal.
