## Summary

Phuket is the gateway to spectacular cruising waters in Southeast Asia, from day trips through limestone karst seascapes to extended passages along the Andaman coast and into Malaysia. The northeast monsoon (November to April) delivers calm seas and steady trade winds, making this a world-class yacht destination.

## Key Information

**Day Trips from Phuket**

Phang Nga Bay, 15-25 nm northeast of Phuket's marinas, is the closest cruising ground. Per Asia Global Yachting and SailScanner (2025), highlights include Khao Phing Kan (James Bond Island), Hong Islands with sea caves, and Ko Phanak's lagoons. Coral Island (Ko Hae), 12 nm south, offers snorkelling. The Racha Islands, 15-25 nm south, provide clear-water bays with sand holding.

**Weekend Trips (2-3 Days)**

The Phi Phi Islands, 25-35 nm east, are a flagship weekend trip. Per SailScanner (2025), Tonsai Bay offers moorings and restaurants; Phi Phi Leh features Maya Bay. Krabi's Railay Beach, 40 nm east, delivers dramatic cliffs and anchorages. Ko Lanta, 50 nm southeast, is a quieter alternative. The Similan Islands, 55-60 nm northwest, offer pristine diving but are seasonal. Per 5 Star Marine Phuket (2025), they open mid-October and close mid-May.

**Extended Cruises (5-10 Days)**

Koh Lipe and Tarutao, 150 nm south near the Malaysian border, offer remote anchorages. Per Breezada (2025), a northward cruise to Ranong is emerging. A 7-day Phang Nga loop from Ao Po covers Ko Phanak, Ko Hong, Railay, Phi Phi, and Racha Yai with daily legs of 10-35 nm.

**International Routes: The "Milk Run"**

Per Breezada (2025), Phuket to Langkawi is approximately 130 nm over three to five days, stopping at Phi Phi, Ko Lanta, and Koh Lipe. Langkawi to Penang is 70 nm; onward to Singapore through the Strait of Malacca is roughly 600 nm total from Phuket. The corridor is well-charted with sheltered stops, though the Singapore approach has heavy traffic.

**Seasonal Considerations**

Per Asia Global Yachting and SailScanner (2025): the NE monsoon (November-April) brings calm seas and E-NE trade winds of 10-15 knots -- prime season. The SW monsoon (May-October) brings squalls and swell; stay on the sheltered east side of Phuket and within Phang Nga Bay. Tidal range is 2-3 metres with currents under 2 knots.

**Protected Anchorages vs. Marina Stops**

Phuket hosts four major marinas: Yacht Haven, Ao Po Grand, Royal Phuket Marina, and Boat Lagoon, per SeaRadar (2025). Arrive early to assess anchorages as many are monsoon-exposed. Mooring buoys must be used in reef areas. Anchoring on coral is a criminal offence.

**Thai Marine National Parks**

Per Asia Global Yachting (2026), marine parks require advance permits and daily fees (approximately $8.50/adult, $4.25/child, $3-$12/vessel/day). Zero-waste policy enforced. Similan and Surin close mid-May to mid-October, per Thai PRD (2025). Phi Phi, Hong Island, and Phang Nga Bay remain open year-round with anchoring restrictions.

## Asian / Thai Market Context

Phuket's cruising grounds are unmatched in Southeast Asia for variety within short distances. Professional agents handle permits and experienced captains know local hazards. Anchorages are less crowded than the Mediterranean, though Phi Phi and Phang Nga Bay see tourist boat traffic in peak season.

## Talking Points for Sand

- Phang Nga Bay is the essential day-trip destination: depart early to secure prime anchorages.
- The Similan Islands are seasonal only, accessible mid-October to mid-May.
- The 7-day Phang Nga loop is the best introduction to Andaman cruising.
- Phuket to Langkawi is a manageable 130 nm with sheltered stops.
- Check marine park permits and seasonal closures before departing.

## DO NOT

- Recommend visiting the Similan Islands during the closed season (mid-May to mid-October).
- Suggest anchoring on coral reefs or within restricted zones of marine national parks.
- Describe any specific anchorage as completely safe without noting seasonal wind exposure.
- Name specific yacht owners or reference their cruising patterns.

## Research Gaps

- Chart coverage of the Mergui Archipelago (Myanmar) for private yacht visits is limited.
- Marine park permit processing times are not consistently published.
- Fuel and provisioning at remote northern Andaman anchorages is poorly documented.
