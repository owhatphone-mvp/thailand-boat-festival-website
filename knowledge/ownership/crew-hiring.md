## Summary

Building a reliable crew is one of the most consequential decisions a yacht owner makes in Asia. The region offers Thai hospitality-trained staff for deck and interior roles, while senior officers are typically recruited internationally.

## Key Information

**Typical Crew Complement by Yacht Size**

Per YPI Crew and YachtBuyer (2026), standard crew numbers:

| Yacht Length | Typical Crew | Common Roles |
|---|---|---|
| 15-20 m (50-65 ft) | 2-3 | Captain, Deckhand/Steward, Chef |
| 20-30 m (65-100 ft) | 4-6 | Captain, First Mate, Engineer, Chef, 1-2 Stewards |
| 30-50 m (100-165 ft) | 8-12 | Captain, First Officer, Chief Eng., Chef, 2-3 Stewards, 2-3 Deckhands |
| 50 m+ (165 ft+) | 12-20+ | Full bridge, engineering, interior departments, Bosun |

**Key Roles**

The Captain holds responsibility for navigation, safety, and compliance. The First Officer/Mate manages deck operations and watchkeeping. The Chief Engineer maintains engines, generators, and mechanical systems. A Chef is expected on yachts above 20 m. Stewards handle hospitality. Deckhands manage tenders, lines, and exterior maintenance.

**Where to Find Crew**

Reputable agencies placing crew in Asia include Yotspot, Crew and Concierge, Luxury Yacht Group, and Camper and Nicholsons, per the Superyacht Services Guide (2025). Facebook groups like "Phuket Yacht Crew" list vacancies. Headhunters in Antibes and Fort Lauderdale also recruit for Asia.

**STCW Certification Requirements**

Per YPI Crew and Bluewater Yachting (2026), STCW Basic Safety Training is mandatory for all professional crew. It covers Personal Survival, Fire Prevention, First Aid, Personal Safety, and Security Awareness. An ENG1 medical is required, with refreshers every five years. Vessels above 24 metres may require additional certifications. Officers need MCA or flag-state Certificates of Competency.

**Thai Crew vs. International Crew**

Thai nationals are widely employed in deckhand, steward, and chef roles across the Phuket fleet, bringing hospitality skills and local knowledge. Per Jobsdb (2026), captain positions are filled predominantly by European, Australian, or NZ nationals with MCA licenses. The typical structure: a Western captain with a mixed Thai-international crew.

**Salary Ballpark Ranges (Monthly, USD)**

Per YPI Crew Salary Guide and Morgan and Mallet (2026):

| Role | 20-30 m | 30-50 m | 50 m+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Captain | $5,000-$8,000 | $10,000-$15,000 | $15,000-$25,000+ |
| First Officer | $3,000-$5,000 | $5,000-$8,000 | $8,000-$12,000 |
| Chief Engineer | $4,000-$6,000 | $6,000-$10,000 | $10,000-$15,000 |
| Chef | $3,000-$5,000 | $5,000-$8,000 | $7,000-$12,000 |
| Steward(ess) | $2,000-$3,500 | $3,000-$5,000 | $4,000-$7,000 |
| Deckhand | $1,800-$2,800 | $2,500-$4,000 | $3,000-$5,000 |

Thai nationals earn at the lower end; internationally recruited officers command premiums.

**Thai Work Permits for Foreign Crew**

Per the Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, foreign crew require a Non-Immigrant B visa and work permit. The yacht's Thai agent sponsors the application (two to four weeks). Enforcement has tightened; crew without permits risk fines and deportation. The DTV visa is not suitable for yacht crew.

## Asian / Thai Market Context

Phuket is Asia's primary yachting hub with a mature crew ecosystem. Thai crew are valued for work ethic and hospitality training. Labour costs are lower than the Mediterranean, but senior officer salaries have risen. Recruit early; turnover peaks during high season.
