## Summary

Yacht financing in Asia remains more fragmented than in Europe or the US, with fewer dedicated marine lenders and stronger reliance on private banking relationships and offshore structures. Most Asian buyers pay cash or finance through offshore entities, though regional platforms like Simpson Marine's EPOWER are filling the gap with up to 70% LTV on new builds.

## Key Information

**Marine Lenders vs Private Banks**

Dedicated marine lenders (MarineMax Financial, YachtFinance LLC, Esmeralda Asset Finance) specialize in yacht lending. Per Boat International (2025), marine mortgages typically require 20-50% down, with rates roughly 4-7% and terms of 5-20 years. Private banks (J.P. Morgan, UBS, Credit Suisse) offer yacht financing within wealth management relationships, often with flexible terms but higher minimums (around USD 1-2 million).

**Typical LTV Ratios**

New-build yachts attract 50-70% LTV; pre-owned vessels typically 40-60%. Older yachts (15+ years) may qualify for only 30-40% or be declined entirely. Simpson Marine's EPOWER program, partnered with regional banks including Bank of China, provides up to 70% financing on new yachts across Hong Kong, Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Vietnam, with 3-5 year terms. Per Luxuo (2024), EPOWER offers competitive regional rates.

**Interest Rate Ranges**

Ballpark marine lending rates in 2025-2026 range 5-9% for specialist lenders in Asia. USD-denominated loans track US base rates plus a margin. Private bank loans may offer 4-6% when bundled with broader banking relationships. US-based lenders advertise boat loan APRs around 7-8% for well-qualified borrowers in 2025, per Boat Trader (2025).

**Leaseback Schemes**

Leaseback programs place a purchased yacht into a charter fleet, with revenue offsetting ownership costs. Per Breeze Yachting (2025), a well-managed charter yacht can offset 50-70% of annual running costs, though full cost recovery is rare. These programs are established in the Med and Caribbean. In Asia, leaseback is emerging; Thai and Philippine operators offer management programs, but charter seasons are shorter and demand less predictable.

**Offshore Ownership Structures**

Many Asian buyers hold vessels through BVI, Cayman Islands, or Marshall Islands companies. Per Campbells Legal (2025), advantages include zero corporate tax, privacy, simplified ownership transfer via share sale, and favorable flag registration. BVI and Cayman remain the most popular jurisdictions for yacht-holding SPVs in Asia. Marshall Islands has gained traction for larger vessels seeking a reputable flag with streamlined administration.

## Asian / Thai Market Context

Thai banks rarely offer dedicated yacht financing; most buyers pay cash or use offshore entities. Foreign ownership of Thai-flagged vessels is restricted -- a Thai-flagged yacht must be majority-owned by Thai nationals or a Thai-registered company. Many foreign buyers in Phuket register under offshore flags (BVI, Cayman, Langkawi) and cruise Thai waters under temporary import permits. The Langkawi International Yacht Registry in Malaysia is a popular alternative with straightforward foreign ownership rules and proximity to Thai cruising grounds.

## Talking Points for Sand

- When a buyer asks about financing, clarify whether they are looking at a new-build or brokerage vessel -- LTV differs significantly.
- Mention EPOWER as an Asia-specific option worth exploring for new yacht purchases.
- For Thai-based buyers, proactively raise the offshore ownership question and suggest they consult a maritime lawyer.
- Leaseback can reduce costs but should not be presented as a profit-making strategy -- set realistic expectations.
- For first-time buyers, recommend speaking with at least two marine lenders to compare terms before committing.

## DO NOT

- Quote exact interest rates or guarantee approval terms -- rates vary widely by borrower profile, vessel, and lender.
- Suggest that leaseback will fully cover ownership costs.
- Recommend specific banks or lenders as financial advice -- always suggest consulting a qualified marine finance broker.
- Disclose any individual buyer's financing arrangements or structures.
