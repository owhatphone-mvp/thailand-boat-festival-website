## Identity

Sunreef Yachts is a Polish luxury catamaran builder founded in 2002 by Francis Lapp, headquartered in Gdansk. The brand pioneered the luxury flybridge catamaran concept in 2003 and has grown into a leading name in semi-custom multihulls, building sail, power, and Eco (solar-electric) catamarans from 60 to 100 feet. Per Yacht Style, the company targets EUR 1 billion in annual revenue under its Beyond 2030 plan.

## Lineup (current models, 2024-2026)

**Sunreef 60**: Entry to semi-custom luxury. Sail and power configurations with personalized layouts.

**Sunreef 70**: Versatile mid-range platform for crewed or owner-operated use.

**Sunreef 80**: The volume leader. $800k to $1.2M USD depending on spec, per YachtWorld, 2025. The 80 Power Eco integrates solar panels into the composite hull and superstructure for silent electric propulsion and reduced generator use.

**Sunreef 100**: Flagship at 30m+ in sail and power. Two 100 Power yachts delivered, per Multihulls World, 2024. Multi-million USD pricing on request.

**Sunreef Eco range**: Features proprietary composite-integrated solar panels described as the world's first system of its kind, per Sunreef Yachts Eco. Ultra-thin, shock-resistant panels are embedded into hull, deck, and superstructure during layup.

## Recent Moves (2024-2026)

Sunreef confirmed a strong presence at TBF 2026 (January, Phuket), per Sunreef's Instagram. The Beyond 2030 plan targets EUR 1 billion revenue with expanded production. The Eco range continues to evolve with over 40 solar catamaran models planned, per Advanced Maritime Technology International. The brand has expanded its power catamaran lineup alongside traditional sailing models.

## Positioning vs Competitors

Sunreef occupies the semi-custom luxury tier above production builders like Lagoon and Fountaine Pajot. Competitors include HH Catamarans, Privilege Marine, and custom divisions of larger shipyards. Sunreef's key differentiator is its Eco solar technology, which no other catamaran builder offers at comparable scale. Semi-custom build processes mean longer lead times but far greater personalization than production brands offer, per YachtBuyer.
